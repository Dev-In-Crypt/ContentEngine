QUESTIONS
Do I need an API key to start?

No. Two posts run on our key — one before you sign up, one after. After that the app asks for your own key, because from then on the writing is billed to you and not to us.

Can I change it before anything goes out?

All of it. The caption, the hashtags, the call to action, the text on each slide, and the time it goes. You can also ask for alternatives to one field and keep the one you like — nothing is saved until you save it.

Where does it publish?

Instagram and X, from the server, on the schedule you set — your computer can be off. It also writes for LinkedIn, but it cannot publish there: the app refuses rather than pretending, and you export or copy the post instead.

What happens to my keys?

They are encrypted before they are stored and decrypted only in memory when a request needs them. They are never written to a log, and the app never sends one back to your browser — the settings screen shows only whether a key is set, and its last four characters.

Can I take my things and leave?

You can download a ZIP with your account, every post, every slide, your images and video, and your usage history. Deleting the account asks for your password and then removes all of it, files included. Keys are listed in the export as set or not set — never as usable text you might forward.

Is any of this automatic without me?

Only what you queued. Nothing is written, scheduled or published unless you asked for it; the scheduler's whole job is to send at the time you already chose.

QUESTIONS
What can it read?

Public links only — a release page, a changelog, a blog feed, a public repository. It is given no login to anything of yours, and it refuses addresses that point back inside a private network.

Who decides what gets posted?

A person, every time. The app finds candidates and writes drafts; approving one is a human action, and publishing happens after that.

Why did it pick this update?

It shows you. Each candidate carries a verdict, the reason, and the sentence from the source the reason came from — so you can disagree with the evidence rather than with a black box.

What can I show later?

Every approval is written to a journal — what the AI drafted, what the person changed, who signed it and when — and the journal exports to CSV or JSON.

What happens to other people's data?

Only the public page is fetched and only the part that became a candidate is kept — its title, its address, and a short quote. Nothing is passed to another customer, and your workspace is not visible from anyone else's. If you delete the account, the sources, the candidates and the journal go with it.

Can it invent something?

The draft is written from the source text and instructed to add no numbers, names or claims that are not in it. That is an instruction to a model, not a proof — which is exactly why a person approves before anything goes out.

WHAT THIS COSTS
Nothing, to us, today

There is no plan and no checkout in this product: two posts run on our key, and after that it runs on yours. Should paid plans arrive, bringing your own key stays one of the ways to use it — the product is built that way, not persuaded into it.

You pay the model vendor

On your own key, at their price, for what you actually generate. It depends on the model you pick: Claude Sonnet 5 lists at $2 per million tokens in and $10 per million out, and a caption is a few thousand.

The running total is on screen

Today and this month, broken down by model. On OpenRouter that is the vendor's own figure; for other providers it is our estimate from a price table, not an invoice.
